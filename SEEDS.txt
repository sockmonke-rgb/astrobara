LAND SOMEWHERE ELSE. Codes are exact: they round-trip to the same seed
every time, on any device, in any build.

Measured against 9,000 generated sites, not chosen by eye. Each pair sits
near the 33rd and 67th percentile of its band, so they are typical of the
rating rather than sites that barely scraped into it.

Columns: crest = columns in permanent sun · ice = minable ice tiles ·
dist = columns from crest to nearest ice · depth = mean rows down to it ·

NOTES

The hand-authored Shackleton site rates 3 stars, which is what anchors the
scale. Type SHACKLETON to land there.

Ratings weight six factors: distance from crest to ice (0.22) and how much
minable ice exists (0.22) matter most, then crest width (0.20), ice depth
(0.13), lander contents (0.12) and helium (0.11). Star cuts are the 12th,
32nd, 68th and 88th percentiles of that score across 20,000 seeds.

One rule overrides that score, added in V2.11.0: a site with fewer than five
columns in permanent sun is never rated below 3 stars. Arrays only go on
sunlit ground, so a thin crest makes every night a power problem however
generous the ice is — and a weighted average can vote that away. It did:
VAST-GATE-900 scored 0.240 against a 2-star cut of 0.235, on a crest of four,
with ice close and plentiful and a heavy lander. Across a 4,000-site sample
the floor lifts 3% of sites, all of them from 2 stars to 3, and no seed
published above changes rating.

A 5-star site is not a harder version of a 1-star one. It usually fails in
a specific way — ice too far to reach before the first night, or a crest
too thin to carry arrays. The reason line for each seed says which.
